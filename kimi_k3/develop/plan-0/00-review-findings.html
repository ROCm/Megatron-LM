The plan is good and three things in it are wrong. The core-isolation strategy, the config-builder bypass, the router-injection path, the VPP descope, the measured-floor tolerance policy and the reasoning about STE and gradcheck all hold up against the source.

What does not hold up: the AttnRes pipeline transport would silently drop half its gradients, the Muon capacity model is built on a mechanism that does not exist as described, and the ground-truth section is missing the one constant that determines pipeline layout. Two further claims about AITER and TransformerEngine could not be reproduced at the pins. Everything else is detail.

Update, 2026-08-27. The first four feasibility gates have since run on hardware, and they turn three of the findings below from arguments into measurements — see what the gates measured. One finding of my own has been retracted: D5 claimed fla silently ignores the released kernel call's arguments, which was inferred from a signature and disproved by running it.

The plan is now built, P0 through P12. 254 tests pass. It converts both ways against the released checkpoint, resumes bitwise under pipeline parallelism, and matches the release's own KDA module on real weights. Building it produced three more findings that reading could not have — including an optimizer that could not be resumed at all, and a quantisation rule that a quarter of the released weights proved wrong. See what building it found.

What changes the design

Twenty-eight of the thirty-one findings are corrections you would fold into a phase. Three change what gets built.

A1 · A4 — critical

The pipeline payload loses its gradients

Core back-props only output_tensor[0]. A two-tensor AttnRes payload trains happily, the loss falls, and the block residual’s contribution never reaches the stage that produced it.

A6 — critical

The Muon memory model is a floor, not a recipe

dist_muon does shard master weights and momentum across DP. Per-GPU cost is 6 + 8/DP, and expert parallelism already shards 98% of the parameters — neither is in the plan’s table.

B1 · B2 — critical

The ground truth is missing block size 12

Eight residual slots, appended every twelve layers, sized by the sequence length. It sets the pipeline payload, the layout, and the shape of the oracle the plan asks for.

The negative control is the finding

Detaching the block-residual slots does exactly what a two-tensor payload would: they travel forward, the next stage consumes them, and their gradient never comes back. The loss is identical to seven decimal places and the gradients are wrong by 8.1e-4. A loss-only comparison would have called that run correct — which is precisely why A1 was worth raising and why the gate asserts gradient flow rather than loss agreement.

And one retraction of my own

Finding D5 claimed that fla's chunk_kda silently ignores A_log, dt_bias and transpose_state_layout, because they are absent from its signature while it ends in **kwargs. That was inferred from reading the signature and never running the call. It is wrong. fla handles all three deliberately — it even raises when A_log is missing — and perturbing A_log demonstrably changes the output. The proposed fix was wrong too: a signature-name check would have failed on a working library.

The real blocker is narrower and was only visible by running it: fla's KDA backward does not compile on gfx950 (D6). The register keeps D5 struck through rather than deleted.

What building it found

The plan is now built, P0 through P12. Three findings came out of executing rather than reading — and each is something that would have shipped silently.

A15 — high

dist_muon could not be resumed at all

ChainedOptimizer.state_dict() returns a list whenever more than one optimizer is chained, and dist_muon always chains two. The layer-wise optimizer then calls .values() on it. It cannot read what its own state_dict() writes — on the optimizer this project measured at 7.87 B/param and recommends for K3.

A16 — high

The MXFP4 scale rule was wrong

P0 used the OCP formula, which targets E2M1’s largest normal by flooring and clips a group peaking at 3.99 down to 3.0. The released weights settle it: 25.75% of a real expert’s groups peak at magnitude 3.0, which that rule cannot produce.

A17 — low

A guard that cannot fire

Core’s “cannot enable both DeepEP and MoRI” error is unreachable: moe_enable_deepep is processed first and overwrites the backend. Ask for both and you get DeepEP, with only an unrelated deprecation warning.

The predicted bottleneck was not the bottleneck

The plan named the AttnRes mixer as the hot spot — ~120 GB of reads per forward — while insisting the trace decides. It does, and they disagree: at official widths the mixer is 0.1% of device time and the Muon optimizer step is 21%. So no AttnRes performance fix was made; the chunked mixer that did land stands on its memory measurement and a bit-identical forward, not on the trace.

The honest limit is arithmetic rather than scheduling. Two AttnRes slots need 13 layers, and 13 layers at official width is 484 GiB per GPU — 1.7× a whole device. Ranking that mixer is structurally a multi-node measurement, and no better-timed local run will change it.

Two gates that would have passed for the wrong reason

The recompute twin is bitwise 0.0 — which is what a correct recompute path produces, and also what a flag that does nothing produces. The harness now counts calls into tensor_parallel.checkpoint: 0 off, 4 on. Likewise the resume test’s RNG control was inert until dropout was switched on, because nothing else drew from the generator. Both were caught by asking what the control would look like if it were broken.

A · Core mechanics

Verified against the pinned fork. Line numbers are at a1b00d4.

A1
Critical

A multi-tensor pipeline payload silently loses its gradient

backward_step back-props only output_tensor[0] with output_tensor_grad[0]; the code says so itself — “can handle at most one skip connection”. Extra tensors sent over P2P (the distillation use case) are forward-only.

So the plan’s “the schedule carries both prefix_sum and block_residual (and their gradients)” cannot be built as two tensors. The block residual’s gradient would be received and dropped, and training would proceed with a wrong-but-plausible loss. The payload has to be one packed tensor — concatenated along the sequence axis so the P2P shape stays a (seq, mbs, hidden) triple — unpacked inside the block.

megatron/core/pipeline_parallel/schedules.py:451-493 · the comment at :459-460 · shape plumbing at :2164-2183

A2
High

Do not wrap or replace get_forward_backward_func

Core already accepts adjust_tensor_shapes_fn(recv, send) in the 1F1B schedule and applies it right after get_tensor_shapes(). It runs per rank, so a stage can legally declare a different receive shape than its send shape — exactly what a monotonically growing block residual needs.

What is missing is only the binding: train_step passes the hook only when NVIDIA-modelopt distillation is active. Binding it from our own entry point is a few lines. Re-implementing 1F1B is hundreds, and re-breaks on every IFU.

schedules.py:2019 parameter · :2180-2183 application · :1975-2004 get_tensor_shapes · training/training.py:1815-1822, 1853

A3
High

The same hook is asserted None at PP=1 and under VPP

The binding must therefore be conditional on PP > 1, or every tiny single-GPU test — the whole of CI stage 1 — fails on an assertion the moment AttnRes is enabled. The plan’s VPP descope is correct and now has a line number to cite.

schedules.py:631-632 no-pipelining · :949-950 interleaved

A4
Critical

The AttnRes payload is up to 9× a normal pipeline tensor, and nothing is budgeted for it

block_residual is [B·S, K, H] with K growing by one slot every twelve layers. At the last boundary the payload is (1+8)·S·B·H: 117 MB becomes 1.06 GB per in-flight microbatch at S=8192, multiplied by the 1F1B in-flight count.

This is a first-order constraint on pipeline depth and the strongest argument for context parallelism, and it appears nowhere in the plan.

HF Kimi-K3:modeling_kimi_linear.py — KimiLinearModel.forward, _forward_attn_residual · config.json — text_config.attn_res_block_size = 12

A5
High

Fully overriding GPTModel.__init__ is unnecessary and permanent

That constructor spans roughly 190 lines — embedding, four rotary variants, MTP, output layer, weight tying, offloading. Duplicating it means re-porting every upstream change to that surface forever.

The actual requirement — instantiate our block, with no transient core block allocated — is met by scoped rebinding of the module attribute gpt_model.TransformerBlock around super().__init__(), which produces no diff under megatron/** and stays guard-clean. Keep the constructor-spy test either way, and add a pin-contract test so an IFU that moves the symbol fails loudly.

models/gpt/gpt_model.py:34 module-level import · :86-274 __init__ · :209-216 self.decoder = TransformerBlock(...)

A6
Critical

The Muon capacity model is wrong, and every number downstream of it inherits that

The plan reads assert not args.use_distributed_optimizer as “Muon cannot shard optimizer state” and builds a flat 14 B/param model, from which it derives 38.9 TB for 93 layers, a seventeen-node floor, the deletion of the sixteen-node configuration, and the conclusion that no viable full-model recipe exists.

But --optimizer dist_muon selects LayerWiseDistributedOptimizer, which shards master weights and optimizer state across the DP and expert-DP ranks by whole-tensor ping-pong assignment, keeps full gradients in the DDP buffer, and all-gathers parameters after the step. Per-GPU it is 2 + 4 + (4+4)/DP — 7 B/param at DP=8, not 14 — plus the Adam group for non-2-D parameters.

Two qualifications so the finding is not overstated: the 38.9 TB arithmetic is right at DP=1, where there is nothing to shard. What breaks are the conclusions — that no sharded Muon recipe exists, that the flat figure applies at every DP, and that a cluster-total table is the right view at all when expert parallelism already shards 98% of the parameters.

optimizer/muon.py:373-386 · optimizer/layer_wise_optimizer.py:26-40, 105-158 · training/training.py:1668 · training/arguments.py:1546-1555

A7
Medium

Muon’s parameter split needs an explicit K3 policy

Only 2-D non-embedding weights go to Muon; everything else goes to the scalar optimizer. For K3 that puts A_log, dt_bias, the three [12288,1,4] conv weights, every RMSNorm and both AttnRes Linear(7168,1) projections in the Adam group at 8 B/param, while b_proj lands in Muon. The memory model is two-group, and the per-head work must know the split.

Also: is_qkv is detected by the literal name linear_qkv.weight and carries an explicit TODO(deyuf): support MLA. Per-head Muon for MLA and KDA supplies its own head split — a real implementation, not a thin wrapper.

optimizer/muon.py:283-302 split · :274-282 is_qkv + TODO · :335-347 bf16 master wrapping

A8
Medium

QK-clip already exists in core

--qk-clip, --qk-clip-alpha, --qk-clip-threshold and clip_qk(model) ship at the pin, gated on TE ≥ 2.9 and driven by self_attention.clip_qk plus core_attention.current_max_attn_logits. Expose the hook on our MLA module and reuse it. Note the walker traverses model_chunk.module.module.decoder.layers and skips modules without the attribute — KDA layers are skipped for free, but our block must keep a .layers attribute under that exact name.

optimizer/qk_clip.py:8-45 · training/arguments.py:1288-1294 · multi_latent_attention.py:1172-1180

A9
High

Core MLA has no NoPE mode

rope_type accepts only "rope" and "yarn" and raises otherwise, and the MLA forward always applies rotary. K3 asserts use_nope, sets rotary_emb = None, and expands an un-rotated shared k_rot across heads. Its scale is q_head_dim ** -0.5 = 192**-0.5, not 128**-0.5, and the output gate multiplies before o_proj. This is a real subclass with an overridden rotary and scale path, not a config flip.

multi_latent_attention.py:193-215 · transformer_config.py:2311 · HF Kimi-K3:KimiMLAAttention

A10
High

Core LatentMoE is almost what K3 needs, but not quite

postprocess() runs fc2_latent_proj(output) with no normalisation, while K3 applies routed_expert_norm = RMSNorm(3584) to the combined expert output before the up-projection. The plan lists LatentMoE under “core consumed as-is”. The fix is small and clean — subclass MoELayer, override postprocess — but it has to be planned. The shared-expert-overlap assertion the plan mentions is real, and TE is genuinely required for the latent projections.

They are built with parallel_mode="duplicated", so every TP rank holds a full 7168×3584 pair per MoE layer — about 4.7 B parameters replicated per TP rank across 92 layers. Worth measuring before choosing TP > 1, and a candidate upstream proposal later. Not a v1 blocker.

A13
High

The local (non-TE) MLA path cannot be constructed at all

MLASelfAttention unconditionally passes k_channels / v_channels to its core_attention submodule, and core's own DotProductAttention accepts neither and has no **kwargs — so any local MLA spec raises TypeError at construction.

B · Ground truth vs the released model

Read from the released config.json and modeling sources, not from the report.

B1
Critical

attn_res_block_size = 12 is absent from the ground-truth section

Slots are appended at zero-indexed layer_idx % 12 == 0 — layers 0, 12, 24, 36, 48, 60, 72, 84, so eight slots, and the final block runs nine layers. Every pipeline-layout, payload-size and AttnRes test decision depends on this number. The tiny preset’s “block size 2” is fine; the official value was simply never stated.

B2
Critical

The AttnRes math is under-specified for building an oracle

The real mechanism: two mixes per layer — one before attention, one before the MLP — plus one at model output; each mix is a softmax over K+1 slots of a score formed from an RMS-normalised slot dotted with norm.weight * proj.weight; the whole mix runs in fp32; and prefix_sum is reset to None at a block boundary so the residual stream restarts per block. “The block carries prefix_sum and block_residual” is not enough to reproduce that.

B3
High

KDA has two gates and the plan conflates them

f_a_proj(7168→128) → f_b_proj(128→12288) is the low-rank decay gate consumed inside chunk_kda with lower_bound=-5. g_proj(7168→12288) is the full-rank output gate consumed by o_norm = FusedRMSNormGated(128, activation='sigmoid') after the kernel. The plan declares “gate form CLOSED”, describes only the first, then lists “sigmoid output gate” as if it were the same object.

B4
Medium

The pinned chunk_kda kwarg list is incomplete

The release also passes use_qk_l2norm_in_kernel=True — so the q/k L2 normalisation the plan describes as a module step happens in-kernel — plus initial_state, output_final_state=True and cu_seqlens. The signature check must assert the full set, and the FP32 oracle must place the normalisation where the kernel does or the two will never agree.

B5
Medium

A_log’s shape is contradictory between the plan and the code

Modeling code declares Parameter(log(uniform(1,16))) of shape [num_heads] = [96]; the plan asserted the padded [128] form without evidence. Settled by reading 512 bytes of the released shard: it is F32 [128] with A_log[96:] exactly zero. The converter now asserts, then trims on import and zero-pads on export, instead of guessing.

B6
High

Shared experts run on the 7168-wide hidden, not on the latent

Only the routed experts run at 3584. Per-layer shared-expert parameters are 3 × 7168 × 6144 = 132.1 M, not 3 × 3584 × 6144 = 66.1 M. The plan states the intermediate size correctly but never the input width; getting it wrong moves a 12 B slice of the model and breaks checkpoint load.

B7
Medium Confirmed · G2

The MLA LoRA norms do not use rms_norm_eps

q_a_layernorm and kv_a_layernorm are constructed without an eps argument, so they take KimiRMSNorm’s class default. If that default is 1e-6, the plan’s blanket “RMSNorm ε = 1e-5” introduces a parity error on a 1536-wide norm that no kernel tolerance will ever explain.

B8
Medium

The release is multimodal and the text config is nested

KimiK3ForConditionalGeneration: the text config sits under text_config, and the shards carry vision_tower.* and mm_projector.* alongside the language model. The mapping spec must strip the nesting and name the vision prefixes as explicitly skipped — the plan’s “zero unmapped tensors” gate fails otherwise — and account for media_placeholder_token_id = 163605.

B9
Low

Router details worth writing down verbatim

fp32 logits → sigmoid → add e_score_correction_bias for selection only → top-16 → weights gathered from the unbiased scores → renormalise → scale by 1.0, with num_expert_group = topk_group = 1 so no group routing happens despite use_grouped_topk: true. This maps cleanly onto core’s TopKRouter, which is good news worth recording.

B10
Low

Two “full-rank gate” flags, two different modules

use_full_rank_gate is a KDA field inside linear_attn_config; the plan attributes “full-rank sigmoid output gate” to MLA. MLA’s gate happens to be full-rank too, for an unrelated reason (mla_use_output_gate). A test written from the plan’s wording would check the wrong flag.

C · Missing engineering content

Not wrong — absent.

C1
High

There is no performance work in the plan at all

No baseline trace, no bottleneck report, no kernel budget, no perf gate. K3 has a large and entirely predictable non-GEMM bottleneck: the AttnRes mixer materialises (T, K+1, H) in fp32 twice per layer — about 120 GB of reads per forward at production shape — which a fused kernel removes. That deserves a phase with a trace, a ranked list and a budget.

C2
High

No process scaffolding

No status tracker, no gate numbering, no per-phase close-out convention, no correctness ratchet, no banned-warning ratchet, no rules file. For a project that will run for months across IFUs, that is the difference between a plan and a plan people can resume.

C3
Medium

The CI ladder does not test the core mechanisms we ride

Five of them — adjust_tensor_shapes_fn, MoESubmodules.router, gpt_model.TransformerBlock, clip_qk, LayerWiseDistributedOptimizer — are what an IFU breaks first, and it breaks them silently. They need a tripwire test in the cheapest CI stage.

C4
Medium

No parallelism layout math

93 layers is not divisible by any plausible PP degree, AttnRes wants boundaries aligned to multiples of twelve, and context parallelism is the natural mitigation for the payload cost. None of it is worked out anywhere in the plan.

C5
Medium

The twin-run protocol has no owner

It is described but not owned by a tool. The noise band, the comparison statistic and the dataset hash need a committed script, or the protocol drifts between runs and the comparison stops meaning anything.

C6
Low

“Matches the released model” needs a caveat wherever it appears

After dequantize_on_import, a BF16 continued-pretrain run starts from MXFP4-quantised-then-dequantised weights — the original BF16 weights were never released. The plan’s byte-exact-roundtrip caveat is right; the semantic one is missing.

E · Kept as written

Verified and carried into the replacement plan unchanged. Do not relitigate these.

  • E2Bypassing core_transformer_config_from_args(): the substitution is real and would silently discard the config subclass.
  • E3Router injection through the MoE submodule spec — real, verified, clean.
  • E4The VPP descope, which matches an explicit assertion in core.
  • E5Component-specific tolerances from a measured eager-fp32-vs-bf16 floor, three statistics, rationale in the docstring.
  • E6gradcheck only for true-autograd modules and never for STE; STE validated against an explicit fake-quant reference.
  • E7Tiny-preset-by-default, with official presets validated analytically rather than instantiated.

  • E9Checkpoint parity against a deliberately truncated HF reference — the only honest way to anchor a four-layer slice.
  • E10The QAT reading: a8w4 forward with a high-precision STE backward, no Hadamard because SiTU soft-caps, RNE by default, no a8w4 dgrad.

F · Consequences for sequencing

The findings change the gated feasibility phase in four ways. The rest of the phase structure survives.

  1. Measure dist_muon, per group, against DP

    Not a single scalar. The optimizer-memory gate produces a bytes-per-parameter curve for adam, precision-aware adam with the distributed optimizer, muon and dist_muon, split by parameter group.

  2. Add a payload-sizing gate before the pipeline design freezes

    Packed payload bytes per stage boundary and the fp32 mixer temporaries, at tiny and at production width.

  3. Add an external-asset gate that fails closed
